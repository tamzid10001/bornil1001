three problems, three answers

what actually costs you money.

your merchandiser types the same order six times.

buyer email, excel costing, whatsapp to the sample room, pi, store requisition, packing list. six versions of one order, five chances to lose a digit — and no one number anyone trusts.

the answer: extraction, then confirmation.

বর্ণিল-RMG reads the tech pack, the po and the challan and fills the fields once. every value keeps a link to the line it came from, so a merchandiser confirms in seconds instead of retyping for an hour.

the expensive mistakes all look small on the day.

a quote sent under the floor margin. fabric booked before the btb opened. bulk cut before the pp meeting. a b/l dated two days past the lc. none of them look like a crisis until the claim arrives.

the answer: four gates that do not open on their own.

each gate is a hard stop tied to a named approver. বর্ণিল-RMG can prepare everything up to the gate — it can never pass one. every release is stamped with who, when and on what evidence.

you hear about monday’s problem on thursday.

by the time the shortage, the machine breakdown and the slipped shipment reach the owner, the overtime is already booked and the air freight is already quoted.

the answer: an exceptions feed, not a dashboard.

one screen on your phone that only shows what broke its own rule — with the order, the amount and the person who can fix it. nothing that is going fine ever appears on it.

the trust loop

propose. approve. commit.

01propose

বর্ণিল-RMG drafts the document with every field traced back to the line it was read from. nothing is saved.

02approve

a named person with the right to that decision reads the draft and presses approve — or edits it first.

03commit

only then does it enter the factory, stamped with who approved it, when, and exactly what they saw.

nothing বর্ণিল-RMG writes enters your factory without a named person’s approval. every step is audited, and the audit shows the draft exactly as the approver saw it.
